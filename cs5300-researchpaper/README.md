# Literature Review of ISAM Software: Capabilities, Constraints, and Configurability within Diverse Orbital Domains

## Thesis Statement

In-Orbit Servicing, Assembly, and Manufacturing (ISAM) is a revolutionary field focused on facilitating the maintainability, longevity, and stability of satellite systems. Primarily focused on Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) and Geostationary Orbit (GEO), space industry efforts to innovate scientific research, refueling, repairs, and self-sustaining constellations have significant traction. However, the industry as a collective has struggled to dedicate a streamlined analysis of which software-related capabilities, constraints, and configurations should be available. This research paper delves into a literature review to analyze advancements from modern space companies and software engineering considerations of integrating AI with human-in-the-loop (HITL) approaches. In addition, this paper highlights the necessary progression required for ISAM to be feasible in different orbital domains.

## Research Questions

RQ1: What software capabilities does current ISAM literature identify as necessary for autonomous assembly, servicing, repair, and lifecycle management of space systems?

RQ2: How do the operational characteristics of LEO, GEO, and emerging cislunar orbital domains create different software-engineering constraints for autonomous ISAM systems?

RQ3: What configurability mechanisms and AI–human-in-the-loop approaches are proposed to let ISAM software safely adapt to changing hardware, mission, and orbital conditions?
